More Competition on the YEG–Puerto Vallarta Route Means This for Snowbirds

By Aunt Tia · November 2026

WestJet has flown direct Edmonton–Puerto Vallarta for a while, and Flair flies it too. This month Porter joined them — a third airline now competing head-to-head on the same nonstop route. For snowbirds who book one-way south and one-way home months apart, more carriers on one route means more chances to catch the cheaper seat on whichever airline is sharpening its price that week. Here's what that competition is actually producing right now.

You don't need a round trip to escape winter — you need one good flight south and the confidence to book your way home separately, whenever you're actually ready.

What three airlines on one route actually means

A search for YEG→PVR direct on November 21 currently turns up all three carriers within $10 of each other: WestJet at $208, Porter at $212, Flair at $218. That tight a spread isn't a coincidence — it's what happens when three airlines are watching each other's prices on the same nonstop route. For a snowbird booking one-way, that competition is the whole point: you're not locked into one airline's round-trip pricing, so you can just check whoever's cheapest on your actual travel date, south in November and home again on a completely different date in the spring.

WestJet, Porter and Flair fares above are from a live meta-search snapshot and will move — they're here to show the competitive spread, not to be quoted as today's price. See Porter's own fare tiers below for numbers checked directly with the airline.

Don't book Basic by accident

Basic is $25 cheaper than Standard but doesn't include a carry-on bag — only a personal item. For a trip south that's likely weeks or months long, that's rarely a real saving once you add a checked or carry-on bag back at the airport. Standard is the one most snowbirds actually want.

The math that makes one-way worth it

If you're booking a return date you're not sure about yet — common for snowbirds waiting to see how long they'll actually stay — two one-way Standard fares (north and south, possibly on different airlines or dates) can beat locking into a round trip with change fees attached. Price both ways before you commit: sometimes round trip wins, sometimes splitting it does. The direct route is what makes that comparison worth doing at all now.
